A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Downloads

| File | For |
| --- | --- |
| `agent-wars-2.21.3-windows-x64.zip` | Windows, x64 |
| `agent-wars-2.21.3-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS on Apple silicon |
| `agent-wars-2.21.3-linux-x64.tar.gz` | Linux, x64 |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | SHA-256 checksums for the three archives |

These are unsigned side-load builds, so Windows and macOS warn before the first launch.

- **Windows:** unzip, then run `Agent Wars v2.21.3.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** unzip, then open `Agent Wars v2.21.3.app`. If macOS blocks it, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Linux:** extract the archive, then run `./agent-wars`. That launcher handles Chromium's sandbox when the archive can't keep `chrome-sandbox` owned by root. On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, where AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces, it starts without the sandbox and says so.

To check a download, compare its SHA-256 with the line in `SHA256SUMS.txt`: `Get-FileHash <file>` in PowerShell, `shasum -a 256 <file>` on macOS, or `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` on Linux.

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **The map is in the download.** Shaded relief is packed with the app, so the map does not use a tile server. The first time a regional map is shown, its tiles unpack on this computer. A commander's model calls still need an internet connection.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
- **Saves.** A save from 2.12.0 through 2.21.2 still opens. A save from 2.10.0 or earlier will not load in 2.21.3.

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 2.21.3

### Turn reports

These lines appear in the turn-update toast and in the Reports tab's copy of that turn report. They name hexes by the same codes commanders use in orders (`orderTargetHexCode`): `AA` on the world map, and `AA.B0` in a battle. The codes are sorted and joined with a serial comma (`formatTurnReportHexList`). A hex with no code reads "an unidentified hex".

- **One missile line.** Visible missile strikes on hexes a side controlled are now one sentence for that side (`buildMissileStrikeToastLines`), in place of one sentence per strike named by place. It reads `Missile strikes in AA and C2 destroyed 2 city cells and 1 airport.`, or `Missile strike in` for a single launch. It totals city cells, airports, seaports, and fortifications, and leaves out a kind with none. When nothing was destroyed it ends `destroyed nothing.` When squadrons were lost it adds `; 1 squadron lost` or `; N squadrons lost`. Strikes on Neutral and uncontrolled hexes share one sentence, which stays in Updates. Which strikes you can see has not changed.
- **One fortification line.** Fortification hits on hexes a side owned are now one sentence for that owner (`buildFortificationToastLines`), in place of one `Fortifications at {place} absorbed N hits.` sentence per hex. It reads `Fortifications in AA and C2 absorbed 3 hits.`, or `hit.` for one. It does not say how many remain. It sits in that owner's section after the missile sentence and before the hex sentences.
- **Battles.** A fortified core cell that absorbs ranged fire or an air strike in a battle is now listed in that beat's report (`createTacticalFortificationHooks`). Before, the absorbed hit went unreported. The beat uses the same fortification sentence, one entry per cell, with cell codes such as `AA.B0`. A battle has no fog, so the opponent's absorbed hits show too. A battle beat has no missile line.

### Commanders

- **Longer memory.** The `## Recent Turn Notes` section of a commander's briefing now covers up to ten prior turns (`RECENT_TURN_NOTES_LIMIT`, 10), up from three. That applies to strategic briefings and to tactical beat prompts, where the periods are that battle's beats (`buildRecentTurnNotesSection`). The section reads the ten most recent stored turns before the one being planned, drops a turn with no message, strategy, or loss, and is left out when none remain. Each block keeps its shape, oldest first: `Turn N:`, then `- Message:`, `- Strategy:`, and, when there were any, `- Enemy Losses:` and `- Your Losses:`. The missile and fortification sentences above are not part of these notes.

## Known limits

- There is no in-app list of saves, and no turn replay.
- Settings, Help, How to play, the Build and Forces tabs, and Replay on a turn report are placeholders in this version.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- On the world map, outside a battle, a route or a played-back move that crosses the 180° meridian runs off the edge of the map. The destination hex is drawn on the opposite edge.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
