A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **The map is in the download.** Shaded relief is packed with the app, so the map does not use a tile server. The first time a regional map is shown, its tiles unpack on this computer. A commander's model calls still need an internet connection.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
- **Saves.** A save from 2.12.0 through 2.25.0 still opens. A save from 2.10.0 or earlier will not load in 2.26.0.

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 2.26.0

This release is about the AI commanders. Nothing new appears in the app's own panels: operations and posture show up in what the commanders are told, in how they reply, and in what their units do on the map.

### Operations

- **The engine runs sea and air operations.** An operation is several turns of orders that carry ground units to an enemy city. The engine writes those orders itself, every turn, whether or not the commander is consulted (`advanceOperationsAfterTurn`, after fog refresh and before the turn is saved). Each operation moves through `muster`, `transit`, `assault`, and, when it turns back, `return`. It ends as completed, lost, or abandoned. Operations belong to AI seats. A commander sees and commands them on strategic consults when standing orders are on.

- **How many run at once.** Each seat may run one sea operation and one air operation on a Small map (`OPERATION_MAX_SEA_PER_SIZE`, `OPERATION_MAX_AIR_PER_SIZE`). Both are multiplied by `GAME_SIZE_MULTIPLIER`: 2 on Medium, 3 on Large. The two caps are separate, so a full sea slot does not block an air search. Under blitz the air cap is doubled.

- **What a sea operation picks.** It needs a free ship with no cargo and no enemy unit within naval range, and at least `OPERATION_MIN_GROUND_UNITS` (2) free infantry or armor. Campaign home defenders, and ground units within `CAMPAIGN_CONTACT_RADIUS` (1) hex of an enemy, are left alone. The muster port is one of up to `OPERATION_MUSTER_CANDIDATE_LIMIT` (6) of the seat's own seaports, within `OPERATION_MUSTER_MAX_LAND_STEPS` (8) land steps of the troops. Each ship carries two infantry or one armor (`fillShip`). Target cities are ranked by city cells, cities with no enemy unit within `LIFT_OBJECTIVE_CLEAR_RADIUS` (2) hexes come first, and at most `OPERATION_OBJECTIVE_TEST_LIMIT` (8) are tested for a sea route.

- **How much of the army can go by sea.** With a land campaign running, a seat lifts at most a quarter of its ground units (`LIFT_MAX_GROUND_SHARE`, 0.25), counting units already aboard or in an operation. Without a campaign the share is half (`LIFT_MAX_GROUND_SHARE_NO_CAMPAIGN`). A maritime posture raises the campaign share to half.

- **A sea operation, step by step.** In `muster`, the units march to the port and the ships gather there. The force loads once every unit and a ship are at the port. After `OPERATION_MUSTER_TIMEOUT_TURNS` (10) turns it loads with whoever has arrived, if that is at least 2 units, or gives up. In `transit` the ships sail to the landing beside the city that is the shortest sail away. A ship that arrives unloads, and its cargo marches on the city. The voyage turns back when the city is no longer an enemy city, or after `OPERATION_TRANSIT_TIMEOUT_TURNS` (20) turns. In `assault`, units with no order are sent at the city until the seat controls it, which completes the operation. After `OPERATION_ASSAULT_TIMEOUT_TURNS` (6) turns the operation ends and the units keep the march they have. In `return`, the ships sail to the nearest home port they can reach, unload, and defend there.

- **What an air operation does.** An air unit at one of the seat's controlled airports carries one infantry unit (`AIRLIFT_INFANTRY_CAPACITY`) to an enemy city that has no enemy unit on it and no known fortification. The infantry must be within 8 land steps of the air unit. In `muster` the infantry marches to the airport and boards. In `transit` the air unit ferries to an airport in drop range of the city when it must, then drops on a later turn; it never ferries and drops on the same turn. On the turn it drops, the operation's ferry or drop replaces any strike, ferry, or drop already ordered for that air unit. The air unit holds fire for the whole operation. If an enemy unit reaches the city, the city stops being an enemy city, or `OPERATION_AIR_TRANSIT_TIMEOUT_TURNS` (6) turns pass, the infantry is unloaded where the air unit stands. Once the infantry is on the city it defends there until control passes, or for 6 turns. Every ending returns the air unit to `defend`.

- **Lost or abandoned.** An operation ends as lost when a unit or ship it still counts on is gone from the map: a sunk ship, a destroyed force, an air unit shot down. It ends as abandoned when it ran out of time, found no landing, or the commander took its units out. A muster left with no ship or no ground unit follows the same split: lost when one of them is gone from the map, abandoned when the commander's own orders emptied it. A return that brings every ship home is abandoned, and one with no ship left is lost.

- **Engine launch.** When a seat has a free slot and a sea or air row it counts as clear, the engine waits one turn and then launches the first such row in menu order, at most one per turn (`decideOperationOffer`). A sea row is clear when no enemy unit is within 2 hexes of the city. Under blitz a sea row with one defender also counts (`engineLaunchMaxDefenders`). Every air row is clear, since an air row is only listed for an empty city. Defender counts include enemy units that fog hides from the commander. The engine never launches a land row on its own. A launch by the commander resets the wait, so the engine does not launch a second row the next turn.

- **Operations bring the commander in.** The turn an operation changes phase or ends, the turn a menu first appears, and the turn a clear row first appears, an event-driven commander is consulted (`operation_update`). That gives the commander a turn to choose before the engine launches.

### What commanders can do

- **Launch, name a city, or cancel.** A new optional `operations` array in the orders reply holds the commands. It is read on strategic consults only.
  - `{"action":"launch","option":"<letter>"}` launches a row of Operation Options. The letter is `A` through `E` (`OPERATION_OPTION_LETTERS`) and names the row as the briefing printed it.
  - `{"action":"launch","objective":"<hex code>","method":"land"}`, or `"sea"` or `"air"`, launches against a city that is not listed. A named sea city is tested even when it is defended. A named air city is refused when it is occupied or fortified.
  - `{"action":"cancel","id":"<operation id>"}` calls an operation off. A sea operation still mustering frees its units and ships. One at sea turns back. One already ashore stops giving orders. An air operation ends and the infantry is unloaded or its march cancelled.

  At most one launch per turn is kept (`normalizeOperationCommands`). A bad entry does not reject the rest of the reply. The refusals name the problem, for example `operations launch dropped: option C is not listed this turn.`, `operations launch dropped: you already have as many sea operations as you can run.`, `operations cancel dropped: <id> is already returning.`, and `operations cancel dropped: the land campaign cannot be cancelled; launch a land option to give it a new target.` A malformed entry gets the three shapes back. Operation refusals are shown once, on the next turn, as a `- Last turn:` line at the top of Active Operations.

- **Turn the land campaign.** With a campaign running, Operation Options can list up to `OPERATION_MENU_LAND_ROWS` (2) land rows: other enemy cities within `CAMPAIGN_MAX_LAND_STEPS` (12) land steps, not the current target. Launching one gives the campaign that target, the same as `campaignTarget`. It is not stored as an operation, does not use a slot, and cannot be cancelled. A launch that names a city with `"method":"land"` does the same. The Campaign rule block now adds: "You may instead launch a land row of Operation Options; both do the same thing."

### Posture

- **Four postures.** A commander can set overland, maritime, strangle, or blitz with a new optional `posture` field in its orders reply (`"posture": "<optional posture>"`, strategic consults only). The engine remembers it per seat. It holds for `POSTURE_MIN_HOLD_TURNS` (6) turns before it can change. An early change is refused with `posture dropped: you set <posture> on turn <n> and may change it from turn <n+6>.` A value that is not one of the four is refused with `posture dropped: expected one of overland, maritime, strangle, blitz.` Sending the current posture changes nothing. A seat that never sets one is on overland (`DEFAULT_POSTURE`).

- **What each posture changes.** The numbers live in `POSTURE_SETTINGS`.
  - **Overland:** the defaults. Operation Options lists land rows first, then sea, then air. Production Priorities asks for ships up to half the naval cap (`NAVAL_PRIORITY_CAP_SHARE`). The lift share is a quarter. The engine launches only against undefended cities.
  - **Maritime:** sea rows first, then air, then land. Production Priorities asks for ships up to the full naval cap. Up to half the ground units may be lifted during a campaign.
  - **Strangle:** Production Priorities keeps naming another city for a missile even when one is already queued, until missiles built and queued reach the missile cap. The city it names for credits must have twice the usual city cells: 24 on Small, 48 on Medium, 72 on Large (`CREDITS_CITY_MIN_URBAN_PER_SIZE` times `GAME_SIZE_MULTIPLIER` times `creditsCityUrbanFactor`). The commander is told to strike city, airport, and seaport cells before units.
  - **Blitz:** air rows first, then land, then sea. The air operation cap is doubled (`airOperationSlotFactor`). The campaign assaults once 3 ground units are massed instead of `CAMPAIGN_ASSAULT_MIN_UNITS` (6) (`campaignAssaultMinUnitsFor`). The engine may launch against a sea objective with one defender.

### Cargo and standing orders

- **Orders on cargo are refused.** An `assign_order`, `cancel_order`, `explicit_move`, `disembark`, `embark`, or `transport_move` on a unit aboard one of an operation's ships or air units is dropped. The refusal says how to get the unit back: `operations order dropped: <unit> is aboard <carrier> in <operation> and takes no order. To take it out, give <carrier> a standing order of your own.` For an air operation the last sentence is `To take it out, cancel <operation>.`

- **Taking units out of an operation.** Any standing order the operation did not assign takes that unit, or that ship, out, and the operation goes on without it. A standing order on a loaded ship takes the ship and its cargo out together, and the cargo stays aboard. An operation emptied this way ends as abandoned.

- **Standing orders do not move units aboard a ship.** This is true outside operations too. A standing march or defend no longer unloads embarked infantry or armor, and that stored order is not advanced while the unit is aboard. `explicit_move`, `disembark`, and an air drop still unload. Embarked units get no suggested destinations, and a unit aboard a ship on a hex where it cannot debark lists no destinations at all.

- **The briefing stops asking for those units.** Units and ships in an operation are left out of Units Without Standing Orders and the orderless-mission count in Attention Flags. They stay in the armed list, since they can still shoot. Units in an operation are also left out of the land campaign's force.

### What commanders are told

- **Operations rule block.** The cached system prompt gains `## Operations` under Strategic Context, after Campaign (`buildOperationRuleBlock`). It opens: "The engine runs operations: several turns of orders that carry ground units to an enemy city by sea or by air." It walks through the sea steps and the air steps, then the menu: "Operation Options lists up to 5 operations you could launch, with what each would gain, what it would tie up, and what stands in its way. A sea or air row is listed only while you have room for another operation by that method." On the engine's launch: "On the turn after a sea or air row was offered, the engine launches the first one it counts as clear: an undefended row, or a lightly held one when your posture allows it. It never launches a land row." On cargo: "A unit aboard one of an operation's ships or air units takes no order of its own: the engine refuses it and says so next turn. A standing order on a loaded ship takes that ship and the units aboard it out together. The operation does not unload those units. An air unit in an operation does not strike; cancel the operation to get it back." It ends: "Outside an operation, a standing order does not move a unit while it is aboard a ship. An explicit_move or a disembark unloads it where the ship is."

- **Postures rule block.** `## Postures` follows Operations (`buildPostureRuleBlock`). "Your posture is what you put your effort into. The engine remembers it and leans production, operations, and the campaign the same way until you change it. Set it with "posture" in your reply. It holds for 6 turns before it can change. If you never set one you are on overland." Each posture gets one bullet with a historical illustration: the Soviet summer offensive of 1944 for overland, the Pacific island-hopping campaign for maritime, the Combined Bomber Offensive and the Battle of the Atlantic for strangle, and the 1940 campaign in France for blitz. It closes: "The historical names are illustrations of an idea. Decide from the map and the tables in front of you, not from how those campaigns went." Without a campaign, the text leaves out the campaign and land clauses.

- **Posture section in the briefing.** Each strategic briefing gains a `Posture` section ahead of Active Operations. It reads `- Posture: overland, the default. You may set another at any time.`, or names the posture, the turn it was set, and `You may change it from turn <n>.` or `You may change it now.` An `- Effect:` line says what the posture changes in this match, for example for blitz: "Air options are listed first and you may run 2 times as many. The campaign assaults with 3 ground units massed instead of 6, and the engine launches against an objective with 1 defender."

- **Active Operations section.** The briefing gains an `Active Operations` section (`buildOperationsSection`). It lists the campaign as `campaign` when one is running, then one bullet per operation by id (`<seat>-op<turn>`), with its phase in plain words. A mustering sea operation says how many of its units and ships are at the port, and one at sea says what is aboard. An air operation says which infantry is marching or aboard, and whether the air unit ferries or drops this turn. Below the bullets: "Give the units and ships named in these operations no orders unless you mean to take them out. A standing order takes out a unit that is not aboard, or a ship. A unit aboard one of their ships or air units takes no order."

- **Operation Options table.** Under Active Operations, `Operation Options` lists up to `OPERATION_MENU_SIZE` (5) rows with the columns Option, Objective, Method, Plan, Impact, Cost, and Risk. Plan names the muster point, the ships or air unit, the landing, and the city. Impact is the city cells and whether the city is in an enemy home region. Cost is how many of the seat's ground units, ships, or air units the row ties up, and about how many turns. Risk is enemy units within 2 hexes, how many more can reach the city in that time, and known fortified cells. Up to `OPERATION_MENU_AIR_ROWS` (2) air rows are kept. The posture decides which method is printed first. When the menu has rows, the consult's opening adds "Launch an operation from Operation Options if one is worth its cost."

- **Response Format.** The reply contract documents both fields when standing orders are on. For `operations`: "To launch a row of Operation Options, add {"action":"launch","option":"<letter>"}. To launch against a city that is not listed, add {"action":"launch","objective":"<hex code>","method":"land"}, "sea", or "air"; the engine builds the operation if your free units can reach it, and the next briefing says why if they cannot. To call one off, add {"action":"cancel","id":"<operation id>"} with an id from the Active Operations section. At most one launch per turn. Omit the array to change nothing." For `posture`: "Optional "posture": one of overland, maritime, strangle, blitz. Omit it to keep your posture. A posture holds for 6 turns before it can change; the Posture section of the briefing says when." The example response carries `"operations": []`. Battle prompts get neither field.

### Other prompt changes

- **Production advice.** A paused queue no longer counts as already building a missile or naval unit, and Production Priorities does not pick a paused city for credits.

- **Mercenary raise flag.** The Attention Flags line for raising mercenaries now names how many to raise, up to what the treasury covers with 3 turns of upkeep left over (`MERCENARY_UPKEEP_RESERVE_TURNS`) and what the city has room for. It reads "Add a raise_mercenaries row with "count": <n> to productionOrders."

- **Missile target flag.** When the best missile target has no city cells, the Attention Flags line calls it an airport or seaport instead of a city with 0 city cells.

- **Steadier cached prompt.** The mercenary rules moved from the turn's Mercenaries section into a Mercenaries subsection of Rules in the cached system prompt. Mercenaries in the briefing now opens with the treasury line. With the weather bonus on, the weather rule and the Weather column stay in the prompt in a mild month instead of dropping out. Long rule paragraphs for air, missiles, fortifications, and weather are split into shorter ones.

#### Campaign

- **Commanders take a city.** On a regional map, with standing orders on, each AI side now keeps one campaign: an enemy city to take, a staging hex near it, and a phase, either assemble or assault. The engine refreshes it once per strategic consult (`resolveCampaignObjective`) and stores it in the `campaign_objectives` match row, which a save keeps. A save without that row starts a fresh campaign on the next consult. The campaign uses only ground units, meaning infantry and armor that are not aboard a carrier. With no ground units, or no enemy city in reach, the side has no campaign that turn.

- **How the target is chosen.** A candidate is a city with at least one urban cell that an active enemy holds. Neutral and knocked-out sides are never targets. Urban counts are public, but control and fortifications count only on hexes the side has explored. An unexplored city inside exactly one enemy home counts as that enemy's, with no fortification penalty (`knownCampaignCities`). Each candidate scores `urban × home weight ÷ ((land steps + 2) × (1 + known forts ÷ 4))` (`scoreCampaignCity`). The home weight is 2 for a city inside an enemy home (`CAMPAIGN_ENEMY_HOME_WEIGHT`) and 1 otherwise. Four known fortified cells halve the score (`CAMPAIGN_FORTIFICATIONS_PER_PENALTY_STEP`). Land steps are counted over land from the nearest of the side's ground units. The engine picks only cities within 12 land steps (`CAMPAIGN_MAX_LAND_STEPS`), and a tie goes to fewer steps, then the lower H3 index (`chooseCampaignTarget`).

- **When the target changes.** A kept target stays until it is no longer a known enemy city, for example once you take it. It also changes when no ground unit can reach it by land within 30 steps (`CAMPAIGN_REACH_LIMIT_STEPS`). The engine then scores the candidates again. So a kept target, or one the commander named, may be up to 30 steps away, while the engine's own pick stays within 12.

- **Staging and muster.** The staging hex is the controlled land hex nearest the target by land, at least 2 steps from it (`CAMPAIGN_STAGING_MIN_STEPS`), that the force can reach. Ties go to fewer steps from the force (`chooseStagingHex`). If the side loses the staging hex, the engine picks a new one. With no qualifying hex, the campaign starts in assault and stays there until the city falls. In assemble, campaign units march to the staging hex and defend there. The assault starts when enough ground units stand within 2 hops of it (`CAMPAIGN_MASS_RADIUS`). That threshold is 6 (`CAMPAIGN_ASSAULT_MIN_UNITS`), or the whole ground force when it is smaller (`campaignAssaultThreshold`). In assault, every campaign unit marches on the target. The campaign falls back to assemble when fewer than half the threshold, rounded up, stand within 2 hops of the staging hex or the target (`nextCampaignPhase`). The mass count includes every ground unit, home defenders too.

- **The assault starts on time.** After a turn resolves, a side whose force has just gathered at the staging hex is consulted that turn (`campaignReadyOverride`, a `campaign_ready` trigger), even if no consult was due. Before this, the assault would wait for the next scheduled consult.

- **Who goes, and who stays.** A ground unit within 1 hex of a visible enemy unit keeps fighting it (`CAMPAIGN_CONTACT_RADIUS`). So does a unit that cannot reach the campaign hex by land. A home guard stays behind (`chooseHomeDefenders`). Each enemy infantry or armor unit within 3 hops of one of your ground units on a home hex counts as a threat (`CAMPAIGN_HOME_DEFENSE_RADIUS`). Each threat keeps one home unit home, nearest the enemy first, then by unit id. The guard is never more than half the ground force, rounded down (`CAMPAIGN_HOME_DEFENDER_MAX_SHARE`), so a side with one ground unit keeps no guard. A home defender whose standing order still marches on the target or the staging hex is told to defend in place.

#### What commanders see

- **A Campaign rule section.** Strategic Context in the system prompt gains a `## Campaign` section (`buildCampaignRuleBlock`). It opens: "The engine picks one enemy city for your ground units to take: the campaign target." It explains the phases, including "Units that arrive one at a time are destroyed one at a time." It also covers the home guard, Campaign Orders, and advance rows. Battle prompts never include it.

- **Campaign Objective.** The briefing has a new Campaign Objective section (`buildCampaignObjectiveSection`), placed before Standing Order Status. Its target line reads like `- Target: T1, a Western Europe city with 54 city cells and no known fortifications. The engine chose it on turn 12.` A commander's pick reads "You chose it" instead. A city whose owner is not known is "an enemy city". The staging line names the hex, or says there is none and the force marches directly. The phase line gives the count, for example `- Phase: assemble. 4 of 6 ground units are within 2 hexes of K2. The assault starts when 6 are.` In assault it says when the campaign falls back. When a guard is held, a Home defenders line lists the unit ids and the number of nearby enemy ground units. It adds "At most half your ground units stay home." when that cap held units back.

- **Campaign Orders.** Standing Order Status has a new Campaign Orders table (`buildCampaignOrderRows`) with the columns Unit ID, Unit Type, Current Order, and Campaign Order. It lists each campaign unit whose standing order should change, and each home defender being recalled. Attention Flags adds "Campaign: N units need a new standing order. Copy each Campaign Orders row into an assign_order entry." This bullet is outside the five-bullet cap. The opening instruction adds "Give each unit in Campaign Orders the order its row names." A campaign unit without a standing order gets the campaign hex as its Suggested Destination, or a defend on its own hex while it waits at the staging hex. A campaign unit that can step onto an enemy this turn is still sent there.

- **Advance rows.** Best Options has a new `advance` action: a one-step move toward the campaign hex, inside this turn's movement budget. A unit already waiting at the staging hex gets no move row. The copy-and-route coaching now names "approach, advance, or move/melee" rows for `explicit_move`.

- **A new `campaignTarget` reply field.** A commander can name a different target by setting `campaignTarget` to an enemy city's hex code in its orders reply. The field is last in the envelope, and only strategic consults on a regional map with standing orders on can use it. A value that is not one two-character code is dropped with "campaignTarget dropped: expected one two-character hex code." A city that is unknown, not an enemy's, or beyond 30 land steps is dropped too, with a reason that names it. Sending the current target changes nothing. A new target gets a fresh staging hex and restarts in assemble (`applyCampaignTargetOverride`). The memory coaching adds "Do not persist the campaign target either; the Campaign Objective section supplies it."

- **Hires lean toward the target.** In Raise Options, cells that would restart a paused city still come first. Next come the cells nearest the campaign target (`compareByCampaignTarget`).

#### Production

- **Credits before walls.** The cap guidance (`PRODUCTION_CAP_QUEUE_GUIDANCE`) no longer says "queue fortification or credits if they are listed". It now reads: "When every unit type a hex can build is at cap, queue credits and spend them on mercenaries. Queue fortification only in a city whose Exposed column says yes, and replace a fortification queue in a city that is not exposed with credits." Controlled Hex Queues has a new Exposed column. It says yes when an active enemy unit, seen or remembered, is within 4 hops (`PRODUCTION_EXPOSED_RADIUS`, `listExposedCityHexes`). The production coaching adds "A city is exposed when an enemy unit you can see or remember is within 4 hexes." The Production attention flag lists credits before fortification, and lists fortification only for an exposed city.

#### Goals and doctrine

- **Taking cities comes first.** On a regional map the goals now come "in priority order": "(1) Take and hold enemy cities. Taking cities is how you knock sides out. (2) Mass your ground units before each attack. (3) Keep enough units at home to stop a raid on your cities. (4) Keep every city building. At your unit caps, bank credits and hire mercenaries. (5) Scout with a few units, not your main force." These replace engaging enemy forces, dispersing for visibility, and maximizing production. Elsewhere the three goals are to destroy enemy forces where you outnumber them, to mass before each attack, and to keep every city building.

- **Plainer strategy lines.** The strategy list in both modes now reads: "(1) Attack where you outnumber the defenders, and avoid fights where you do not. (2) Strike gaps the enemy cannot reinforce quickly. (3) Keep the initiative: move toward your objective every turn." A battle prompt says "beat" instead of "turn". This replaces "speed, surprise, and initiative", "gaps and surfaces", and "maximum disruption with minimum force".

- **Doctrine names the objective.** Balanced now has its own block: "(1) Advance on the campaign target while your home cities are safe. (2) Trade a unit for a city when the odds favor you." Its second line goes on to say a lost unit is usually rebuilt within a turn or two at the caps, and a lost city is not. Aggressive now advances on the campaign target from the first turn. Cautious holds home and the nearby cities and sends the rest to the campaign staging hex. It attacks only from cover or with the full massed force, and prefers a target an enemy is already fighting a third side for. Without a campaign, each block names the nearest enemy home region instead (`buildDoctrineBlock`).

#### Prompt layout

- **Rules in the system message, counts in the situation message.** The system message now holds every rule that does not change during a match. Scenario Objective, the home-region hex lists, and Sides move there from the situation message. `# Combat and Movement Rules` becomes `# Rules`, split into subsections such as Combat, Movement, Fortifications, and Production. The production cost, cap, income, and buildability lines move there from Production Status. A new `# Reading the Briefing` section holds the map legend, distance and intel-age caveats, the Best Options odds key, memory slots, and the air, missile, and transport notes. Coaching leaves the tools section for a new `# Orders Guidance` section, with subsections from Winning through Messages and Rounds. The situation message keeps the clock, the briefing, a new Scenario Progress section with the explored, controlled, home-control, and urban-share counts, the status sections, and Unit Roster.

- **Scouting is always coached.** The scouting line used to appear in the situation message only when no enemy was observed. It is now always under Using the Briefing: "When the briefing shows no observed enemy unit, move to regain contact along plausible approaches rather than holding position."

#### Battles

- **Beats left on the battle header.** An AI-only battle with a beat limit shows ` (N left)` beside the beat number (`aiOnlyBeatsLeft`). N is the limit minus the current beat. It is 0 on the last beat and stays 0 if playback has moved past it. The count turns crimson (`--order-missile`, `#B91C1C`) at 5 or fewer (`AI_ONLY_BEATS_LEFT_URGENT_AT`). Unlimited and None show no count.

- **The commander is told the same count.** In that battle, the commander's Current Situation adds a paragraph after the clock (`formatAiOnlyBeatsLeftPrompt`): "Beats left before this battle ends on its own: N. This count is 0 on the last beat. This beat still resolves, and the battle ends after the beat where the count is 0. Use the beats you have on the fight in front of you."

#### Production

- **Paused points are discarded.** A queue that is still building keeps the points it could not spend, and they carry toward its next item. A paused queue now stores 0 points at the end of the turn (`pointsKeptAfterPass`). Before this release a paused queue kept banking its points. A queue counts as paused only when it has a row or Keep building is on, and only for one of three reasons (`listQueuePauseReasons`). The second is a full city: the next item is a fortification and no city cell is left to fortify. The queue rows and Keep building stay, and an occupation pause still shows ∞t on the map label.

- **One line in the build popup.** The single-hex popup's long occupation paragraph is replaced by one line that names every reason that applies (`formatQueuePausedNote`), such as `Queue paused: occupying units or fortifications required.` The other reasons read `every city cell fortified` and `unit cap reached`. Two reasons are joined with "and". The popup now shows a cap pause and a full-city pause as well as occupation.

- **Named once in the turn report.** Updates names a queue only on the turn it pauses (`selectNewlyPausedQueues`). The engine remembers last turn's paused hexes in the `queue_paused_hexes` match row, which a save keeps. A queue that stays paused is not named again until it builds and then pauses again. Hexes with the same reasons share one sentence and are named by hex code instead of place name: `Queues paused at AA and C2: occupying units or fortifications required.` A cap pause is reported now too, and the separate full-city and occupation sentences are gone. The first turn after loading an older save names every paused queue once.

- **Wider build popups.** Every build popup is at least 200 px wide. A build-only popup and the multi-hex editor are capped at 200 px, up from 150. Hire, and a hex that shows both Build and Hire, may grow to 280 px, up from 210 (`.build-popup-wide`).

#### Commanders

- **The production rule changed.** The income rule (`buildProductionIncomeRule`) now ends: "A queue that is building keeps its unspent points toward the next item. A paused queue loses its points at the end of the turn." The buildability rule (`buildBuildabilityRule`) no longer says a queue "keeps banking its points". Its second paragraph reads: "Elsewhere a queue pauses, and its points are lost at the end of each turn until you end a turn with a unit there or raise a mercenary there."

- **Paused Queues.** Production Status has a new Paused Queues subsection (`SECTION_TITLE_PAUSED_QUEUES`) when a queue is paused for occupation or a full city. A pause from the cap alone is left to the Production attention flag. The subsection uses the turn report's grouped sentences, then "A paused queue loses its production points at the end of each turn." Then it adds one fix per reason present. For occupation: `To clear "occupying units or fortifications required", end a turn with one of your units in the cell, or raise a mercenary there (Raise Options shows yes under Resumes building).` For a full city: `To clear "every city cell fortified", replace that cell's queue with set_build_queue.` Attention Flags adds the pointer "Paused queues: see Paused Queues under Production Status." This pointer does not count against the five-bullet cap. The production coaching bullet adds "When an attention bullet names paused queues, apply the fix in Paused Queues under Production Status."

- **Production tools report the pause.** `query_production` adds two fields for each hex the seat controls. `pausedReasons` lists each reason's text and is omitted when there is none. `storedPoints` is the points carried toward the next item, which is 0 after a paused turn. `set_build_queue` returns the same fields. In Controlled Hex Queues, the Building column now reads `paused` for any reason, including the cap. Before, it read `paused` only for occupation.

#### Map picture

- **Local shaded relief.** The picture under the hexes is a hillshade packed with the app. It no longer comes from Esri World Shaded Relief or OpenStreetMap. Elevation is GMTED2010, and the water mask is Natural Earth. The app serves the tiles itself on the `agentwars-basemap` protocol (`resolveBasemapTile`), so the map draws with no network. Model calls still need one.

Every regional map also ships its own pack for zooms 8 and 9, so a regional match has two more levels of native detail than the world map (`game:basemapNativeZoom` returns 9 or 7). Past that zoom the same tiles are stretched, up to zoom 16. The old Esri layer went to zoom 13, so a close view is softer than before.
- **Unpacking.** The first time a part of the map is shown, its tiles unpack into the app's user-data folder and are reused after that. Entering a battle starts unpacking that battle's tiles in the background (`game:basemapPrefetch`), and the battle does not wait for it. If tiles still fail to load, one map toast per session says `Some map tiles failed to load. The game remains playable; try again later.`
- **Clearer mountains.** Land tiles are drawn at `contrast(1.4)` (`LAND_TILE_CONTRAST`), so ridges stand out. The water and the grid lines do not get that contrast.
- **Elevation-tile lines.** The elevation source is built from tiles 30° wide and 20° tall, and their joins show in the picture. The main map now draws a fine line on each join, in the theme's grid color. The equator, the prime meridian, and the 180° line are solid. The other 30° meridians and the parallels at 10°, 30°, 50°, and 70° north and south are dashed. The minimap does not draw them.
- **No credit line on the map.** The map no longer prints an attribution next to the scale. The elevation and Natural Earth credits are in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`.

- **Four themes.** Standard, Topographic, Nautical, and Night replace the Default, Atlas, Wargame, and Scientific terrain styles. The choice is the dropdown in the terrain legend, which **Legend** opens. Standard is the default: near-white land and soft blue water. A theme sets the water color, recolors the gray land with a filter, and colors the grid, coastline, borders, roads, rails, and place names (`MAP_THEMES` in `src/shared/mapThemes.ts`). The minimap takes the same land and water colors.
- **Hex colors.** Standard, Topographic, and Nautical use the old Default hex colors. Night uses the old Wargame hex colors. The Atlas and Scientific hex colors are gone.
- **Roads and rails.** Night draws road centers in `#8b95a3` and rail dashes in `#dfe6ee`, with dark outlines and halos. The other themes keep white road centers and black rail dashes (`transportLineColors`).
- **Not saved.** Changing the theme does not reload the tiles and does not touch the match, the selection, or queued orders. The choice starts at Standard each time you open the app.

#### Coastlines, borders, and names

- **Coastlines.** A 1.5 px hairline follows every coast and lake shore on the main map (`COASTLINE_WEIGHT_PX`). Past the tiles' native zoom, a land shape under the tiles and a water shape over them keep that edge sharp. The minimap draws only the coarsest coastline, at 1 px.
- **Borders.** National borders appear from zoom 4 (`COUNTRY_LABEL_MIN_ZOOM`), solid, at two thirds opacity (`NATIONAL_BORDER_OPACITY`). Continents are 18 px capitals, countries 13 px capitals, and states 11 px in their own case. At most 150 names draw at once (`MAX_LABELS_PER_FRAME`), and a country or state name that would touch an earlier one is skipped.

- **New-game screen.** The coastline, the borders, and the names stay on under the new-game screen. While it is open, they and the hillshade follow that screen's preview map, not a match or battle waiting underneath. Closing the screen restores the waiting match's hillshade.

#### Climate

- **New climate source.** Monthly weather is CHELSA V2.1 for 1981–2010. It replaces WorldClim 2.1. Every hex, land or water, uses the mean of a 10-arc-minute box around its center. A hex the climate data does not cover copies the nearest covered hex within two strategic steps on the world map, and within 5 or 14 steps on regional maps with smaller hexes. In all, 4,945 of the world map's 10,104 hex-months change. Regional maps get the same new climate.
- **Saves.** Weather is read from the climate pack, not the save, so a match you open uses the new months.
- **Commanders.** No commander prompt changed in this release. The weather in their briefings, and the weather-adjusted ranges they are offered, come from the new climate.

## Known limits

- There is no in-app list of saves, and no turn replay.
- Settings, Help, How to play, the Build and Forces tabs, and Replay on a turn report are placeholders in this version.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- On the world map, outside a battle, a route or a played-back move that crosses the 180° meridian runs off the edge of the map. The destination hex is drawn on the opposite edge.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
