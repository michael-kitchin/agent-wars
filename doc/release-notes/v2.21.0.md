A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Downloads

| File | For |
| --- | --- |
| `agent-wars-2.21.0-windows-x64.zip` | Windows, x64 |
| `agent-wars-2.21.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS on Apple silicon |
| `agent-wars-2.21.0-linux-x64.tar.gz` | Linux, x64 |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | SHA-256 checksums for the three archives |

These are unsigned side-load builds, so Windows and macOS warn before the first launch.

- **Windows:** unzip, then run `Agent Wars v2.21.0.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** unzip, then open `Agent Wars v2.21.0.app`. If macOS blocks it, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Linux:** extract the archive, then run `./agent-wars`. That launcher handles Chromium's sandbox when the archive can't keep `chrome-sandbox` owned by root. On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, where AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces, it starts without the sandbox and says so.

To check a download, compare its SHA-256 with the line in `SHA256SUMS.txt`: `Get-FileHash <file>` in PowerShell, `shasum -a 256 <file>` on macOS, or `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` on Linux.

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **The map is in the download.** Shaded relief is packed with the app, so the map does not use a tile server. The first time a regional map is shown, its tiles unpack on this computer. A commander's model calls still need an internet connection.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
- **Saves.** A save from 2.12.0 through 2.20.0 still opens. A save from 2.10.0 or earlier will not load in 2.21.0. Weather is read from the climate pack, so an opened save uses the new months. A save keeps the city cells it was created with.

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 2.21.0

### Map picture

- **Local shaded relief.** The picture under the hexes is a hillshade packed with the app. It no longer comes from Esri World Shaded Relief or OpenStreetMap. Elevation is GMTED2010, and the water mask is Natural Earth. The app serves the tiles itself on the `agentwars-basemap` protocol (`resolveBasemapTile`), so the map draws with no network. Model calls still need one.
- **Zoom detail.** Zooms 0 to 7 come from a world pack. Every regional map also ships its own pack for zooms 8 and 9, so a regional match has two more levels of native detail than the world map (`game:basemapNativeZoom` returns 9 or 7). Past that zoom the same tiles are stretched, up to zoom 16. The old Esri layer went to zoom 13, so a close view is softer than before.
- **Unpacking.** The first time a part of the map is shown, its tiles unpack into the app's user-data folder and are reused after that. Entering a battle starts unpacking that battle's tiles in the background (`game:basemapPrefetch`), and the battle does not wait for it. If tiles still fail to load, one map toast per session says `Some map tiles failed to load. The game remains playable; try again later.`
- **Clearer mountains.** Land tiles are drawn at `contrast(1.4)` (`LAND_TILE_CONTRAST`), so ridges stand out. The water and the grid lines do not get that contrast.
- **Elevation-tile lines.** The elevation source is built from tiles 30° wide and 20° tall, and their joins show in the picture. The main map now draws a fine line on each join, in the theme's grid color. The equator, the prime meridian, and the 180° line are solid. The other 30° meridians and the parallels at 10°, 30°, 50°, and 70° north and south are dashed. The minimap does not draw them.
- **No credit line on the map.** The map no longer prints an attribution next to the scale. The elevation and Natural Earth credits are in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`.

### Map themes

- **Four themes.** Standard, Topographic, Nautical, and Night replace the Default, Atlas, Wargame, and Scientific terrain styles. The choice is the dropdown in the terrain legend, which **Legend** opens. Standard is the default: near-white land and soft blue water. A theme sets the water color, recolors the gray land with a filter, and colors the grid, coastline, borders, roads, rails, and place names (`MAP_THEMES` in `src/shared/mapThemes.ts`). The minimap takes the same land and water colors.
- **Hex colors.** Standard, Topographic, and Nautical use the old Default hex colors. Night uses the old Wargame hex colors. The Atlas and Scientific hex colors are gone.
- **Roads and rails.** Night draws road centers in `#8b95a3` and rail dashes in `#dfe6ee`, with dark outlines and halos. The other themes keep white road centers and black rail dashes (`transportLineColors`).
- **Not saved.** Changing the theme does not reload the tiles and does not touch the match, the selection, or queued orders. The choice starts at Standard each time you open the app.

### Coastlines, borders, and names

- **Coastlines.** A 1.5 px hairline follows every coast and lake shore on the main map (`COASTLINE_WEIGHT_PX`). Past the tiles' native zoom, a land shape under the tiles and a water shape over them keep that edge sharp. The minimap draws only the coarsest coastline, at 1 px.
- **Borders.** National borders appear from zoom 4 (`COUNTRY_LABEL_MIN_ZOOM`), solid, at two thirds opacity (`NATIONAL_BORDER_OPACITY`). State and province borders appear in the detail view, the close view that draws the smaller hexes, including a battle. They are dashed and at half opacity (`STATE_BORDER_OPACITY`). A disputed or indefinite line uses a short dash. The world view below zoom 4 draws no borders.
- **Place names.** Names are drawn on their own layer, above the hillshade and borders and under the hexes and units. Below zoom 4 the world view shows continent names. From zoom 4 it shows country names, with the continent names behind them at half strength. The detail view shows state and province names, with country names behind them at half strength, and no continent names (`visibleLabelKinds`, `labelKindOpacity`). Continents are 18 px capitals, countries 13 px capitals, and states 11 px in their own case. At most 150 names draw at once (`MAX_LABELS_PER_FRAME`), and a country or state name that would touch an earlier one is skipped.
- **Cities win over states.** A state or province name is left off while a city of the same name is labeled in view, ignoring accents and case (`withoutStatesNamedForCities`). Holding the inspect key (T) does not bring that name back.
- **New-game screen.** The coastline, the borders, and the names stay on under the new-game screen. While it is open, they and the hillshade follow that screen's preview map, not a match or battle waiting underneath. Closing the screen restores the waiting match's hillshade.
- **Fonts.** Standard and Night use Noto Sans. Topographic and Nautical use Noto Serif. Both are bundled with the app.

### Climate

- **New climate source.** Monthly weather is CHELSA V2.1 for 1981–2010. It replaces WorldClim 2.1. Every hex, land or water, uses the mean of a 10-arc-minute box around its center. A hex the climate data does not cover copies the nearest covered hex within two strategic steps on the world map, and within 5 or 14 steps on regional maps with smaller hexes. Past that, it is mild all year. Snow, rain, and heat use the same thresholds as 2.20.0.
- **More weather at sea.** Water hexes used to copy the nearest land hex within two steps, and open ocean was mild all year. Now the sea has its own weather. On the world map, water hexes are mild in 1,731 of 4,920 hex-months, down from 4,816. Land changes too: land hexes are mild in 1,725 of 5,184 hex-months, down from 3,389. In all, 4,945 of the world map's 10,104 hex-months change. Regional maps get the same new climate.
- **Saves.** Weather is read from the climate pack, not the save, so a match you open uses the new months.
- **Commanders.** No commander prompt changed in this release. The weather in their briefings, and the weather-adjusted ranges they are offered, come from the new climate.

## Known limits

- There is no in-app list of saves, and no turn replay.
- Settings, Help, How to play, the Build and Forces tabs, and Replay on a turn report are placeholders in this version.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- On the world map, outside a battle, a route or a played-back move that crosses the 180° meridian runs off the edge of the map. The destination hex is drawn on the opposite edge.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
