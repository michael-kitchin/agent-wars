A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Downloads

| File | For |
| --- | --- |
| `agent-wars-2.8.5-windows-x64.zip` | Windows, x64 |
| `agent-wars-2.8.5-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS on Apple silicon |
| `agent-wars-2.8.5-linux-x64.tar.gz` | Linux, x64 |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | SHA-256 checksums for the three archives |

These are unsigned side-load builds, so Windows and macOS warn before the first launch.

- **Windows:** unzip, then run `Agent Wars v2.8.5.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** unzip, then open `Agent Wars v2.8.5.app`. If macOS blocks it, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Linux:** extract the archive, then run `./agent-wars`. That launcher handles Chromium's sandbox when the archive can't keep `chrome-sandbox` owned by root. On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, where AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces, it starts without the sandbox and says so.

To check a download, compare its SHA-256 with the line in `SHA256SUMS.txt`: `Get-FileHash <file>` in PowerShell, `shasum -a 256 <file>` on macOS, or `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` on Linux.

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **Map imagery needs an internet connection.** Map tiles come from Esri and OpenStreetMap.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 2.8.5

This build carries the 2.8 line. The sections below are the changes from 2.8.0 through 2.8.5.

### 2.8.5

- **Hex control.** The line names the controller with that side's short name. A side holding its own home reads Iraq (home). Another side's home reads Iraq (W. Turkey home). Overlapping homes list every short name in seat order, such as W. Turkey (Iraq & W. Turkey homes). A hex that is not a home names only the controller. Neutral stays Neutral.

### 2.8.4

- **Side names.** A short name that would otherwise be a single initial, or a cut that leaves an unclosed parenthesis, is now a letter form of the full home name. South & east China, Taiwan reads SECT. Tasman (Victoria, Tasmania, NZ) reads TVTNZ. East stays East. The full name of such a home ends with that letter form in parentheses, such as South & east China, Taiwan (SECT). A match saved before the abbreviation was added still shows the name it saved, and the short names stay the letter form.
- **Runtime.** The packaged app runs on Electron 40.10.6.

### 2.8.3

- **Turn report.** After Ready, each side's section says how many hexes that side lost this turn, and to whom. The number is the total for the turn. The other sides are named once, in alphabetical order, and you come first when you are one of them. An opponent's short name keeps its numbered flag in front of the name. A side that only captured hexes has no section of its own. Updates does not list those hex changes. Deployments, and a battle outside your vision, stay under Updates.

### 2.8.2

- **Turn report.** After Ready, each side's section says how many hexes that side lost this turn, and to whom, and how many it took, and from whom. The number is the total for the turn. The other sides are named once, in alphabetical order, and you come first when you are one of them. An opponent's short name keeps its numbered flag in front of the name. Updates no longer lists those hex changes again. Deployments, and a battle outside your vision, stay under Updates. The 2.8.3 section above is the report this build uses: it keeps the losses and drops the lines about hexes taken.
- **New game and game over.** The setup list scrolls to the top whenever that screen opens, and when a match ends while it is already open, so the result is in view. If the choices were hidden, that scroll happens when they are shown. Opening New game during a match clears the previous result.

### 2.8.1

- **Commander popover.** The API key field and the model list start at the same left edge as the reasoning list. Every label is right-aligned. Reasoning and Doctrine, labels and lists, share the line under the model. When a model has no reasoning levels, that list stays on the line, disabled, and reads "Model default".
- **Starting a match.** Start game and New game collapse the console. The tab you had selected stays selected, so the next click on that tab opens it. Planning still follows Auto-start AI: it turns on when that box is checked and at least one commander has a key and a model, and stays off otherwise. Choosing No on the no-model question leaves the console as it was.
- **Observe.** If Orders was selected, the console moves to Commanders. A collapsed console stays collapsed.

### 2.8.0

- **One commander per AI side.** Each card has its own API key, model, reasoning effort, and doctrine. Adding an opponent copies the previous opponent's commander settings, not its home. Observe only gives every side to a commander.
- **Start and Stop.** They sit at the top of the Command tab and apply to every commander. **Start** stays off until at least one commander has both a key and a model. Starting a match with no model selected asks first: "Start the game with no models set?"
- **Ready while commanders plan.** The button reads `AI: MM:SS (xN)`. N is how many commanders are still planning.
- **Messages.** Opponent messages share one dialog, with a tab per AI side. A tab starts disabled and turns on when that side sends a message.
- **Turn update.** Losses are listed by side, with each unit's origin flag, then the hex lines and the other updates. The hex lines in this build are the 2.8.3 report above.
- **How the match ends.** Both maps offer Deathmatch, the default, which has no turn limit, and Most city hexes at turn limit, which lasts 20, 30, 40, or 50 turns.

## Known limits

- There is no player-facing save slot or load list yet. SQLite keeps the match in progress on disk.
- Settings, Help, Save game, Load game, How to play, the Build and Forces tabs, and Replay on a turn report are placeholders in this version.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
