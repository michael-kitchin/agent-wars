A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Downloads

| File | For |
| --- | --- |
| `agent-wars-2.20.0-windows-x64.zip` | Windows, x64 |
| `agent-wars-2.20.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS on Apple silicon |
| `agent-wars-2.20.0-linux-x64.tar.gz` | Linux, x64 |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | SHA-256 checksums for the three archives |

These are unsigned side-load builds, so Windows and macOS warn before the first launch.

- **Windows:** unzip, then run `Agent Wars v2.20.0.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** unzip, then open `Agent Wars v2.20.0.app`. If macOS blocks it, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Linux:** extract the archive, then run `./agent-wars`. That launcher handles Chromium's sandbox when the archive can't keep `chrome-sandbox` owned by root. On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, where AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces, it starts without the sandbox and says so.

To check a download, compare its SHA-256 with the line in `SHA256SUMS.txt`: `Get-FileHash <file>` in PowerShell, `shasum -a 256 <file>` on macOS, or `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` on Linux.

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **Map imagery needs an internet connection.** Map tiles come from Esri and OpenStreetMap.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
- **Saves.** A save from 2.12.0 through 2.19.0 still opens. A save from 2.10.0 or earlier will not load in 2.20.0. A save keeps the city cells it was created with, so a 2.19.0 save still has the date-line cities that a new game no longer has.

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 2.20.0

- **Date-line cities.** A new game no longer draws a vertical line of city cells down the 180° meridian. The terrain build tests each cell for city and water against its outline. A cell that straddles the 180° line used to be unwrapped across the whole width of the world, so it overlapped every city on the way. Each such cell is now split at the map seam, and each piece is tested on its own side (`project_cell_for_masks` in the terrain pipeline). The two cells that hold a pole keep the old test.
- **The global map's economy.** On the world map, 340 battle-scale city cells on that line are gone. The map now has 4,303 city cells, down from 4,643. Nineteen strategic hexes on the line held only those false cells, so they stop producing, and 264 hexes produce, down from 283. Unit prices on the world map do not change.
- **Water and coast on the date line.** The same split corrects the water test. On the world map, 18 strategic hexes on the line are now all water, so they play as water instead of coast or wetlands (`waterRelationFromMaskMetrics`). At battle scale, 523 cells on the line become water. No cell on the world map turned from water to land.
- **Australia & New Zealand.** A new game uses the corrected counts: 234 city cells, down from 238, and 38 producing hexes, down from 39. The map's cost scale drops from 0.480 to 0.446, so every price falls. Infantry costs 9 (was 10), armor 18 (was 20), naval 45 (was 50), air 27 (was 30), and a missile 90 (was 100). Air now needs 3 city cells in a hex, down from 4. The armor and naval minimums, the Advanced threshold, the strike size, and missile range are unchanged.
- **Eastern Europe.** A new game has 1,156 city cells, down from 1,182, and 102 producing hexes, down from 106. The cost scale rises from 0.833 to 0.844. Prices and build minimums stay the same. One strategic air strike on a city now destroys 8 city cells, up from 7 (`strategic_strike_urban_cells` in the map's manifest, read by `strategicUrbanCellsDestroyedPerHit`).
- **Side-group shares.** The share of each map's city cells held by each side group moved by less than two points, and both maps still pass the balance check. On Eastern Europe, Urals, Siberia & Far East drops from 22.6% to 20.8%. The other groups there gain about half a point each. On Australia & New Zealand, Tasman (Victoria, Tasmania, NZ) drops from 33.6% to 32.5%, New South Wales & ACT rises to 27.4%, and Queensland, NT, WA & SA rises to 40.2%.
- **Saves.** A save started in an earlier version keeps the city cells it was created with. Prices and the strike size come from the shipped map when a match loads, so a regional save opened in 2.20.0 uses the new numbers.

## Known limits

- There is no in-app list of saves, and no turn replay.
- Settings, Help, How to play, the Build and Forces tabs, and Replay on a turn report are placeholders in this version.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- On the world map, outside a battle, a route or a played-back move that crosses the 180° meridian runs off the edge of the map. The destination hex is drawn on the opposite edge.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
