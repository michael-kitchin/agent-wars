A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Downloads

| File | For |
| --- | --- |
| `agent-wars-3.1.0-windows-x64.zip` | Windows, x64 |
| `agent-wars-3.1.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS on Apple silicon |
| `agent-wars-3.1.0-linux-x64.tar.gz` | Linux, x64 |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | SHA-256 checksums for the three archives |

These are unsigned side-load builds, so Windows and macOS warn before the first launch.

- **Windows:** unzip, then run `Agent Wars v3.1.0.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** unzip, then open `Agent Wars v3.1.0.app`. If macOS blocks it, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Linux:** extract the archive, then run `./agent-wars`. That launcher handles Chromium's sandbox when the archive can't keep `chrome-sandbox` owned by root. On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, where AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces, it starts without the sandbox and says so.

To check a download, compare its SHA-256 with the line in `SHA256SUMS.txt`: `Get-FileHash <file>` in PowerShell, `shasum -a 256 <file>` on macOS, or `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` on Linux.

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **The map is in the download.** Shaded relief is packed with the app, so the map does not use a tile server. The first time a regional map is shown, its tiles unpack on this computer. A commander's model calls still need an internet connection.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
- **Saves.** A save from 2.12.0 through 3.0.0 still opens. A save from 2.10.0 or earlier will not load in 3.1.0. A save made before 3.1.0 has no statistics and no replay pictures for the turns it already played. Its charts read "No turns have been recorded yet." until you play a turn, and replay starts at the first turn 3.1.0 recorded. A commander stance saved before 3.1.0 has no trust or threat, and shows "no report" on the charts and `not reported` in that commander's prompts until the commander sets it again.

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 3.1.0

### Replay

- **Look back at past turns.** Press **P** during a match, or check **Playback…** in the game menu (uncheck it to return). A panel opens at the map's bottom-left, in the selection panel's place. Its label reads "Replay · Turn N of M", where M is the last recorded turn. Step back and Step forward move one turn. Play runs the turns forward. The arrow at the panel's left opens a turn field and a slider, so you can jump to a turn. Press **P** again or Escape to return to the present.
- **What a step shows.** The map, the top bar, and the panels as they stood when that turn began, with the update dialog and the commanders' message dialog that turn showed. Replay hands back what the match recorded and works nothing out again. A player with a seat sees what that seat could see then. An observer sees every side, and so does anyone once the match is over.
- **Replay changes nothing.** Ready, the Orders tab, and map clicks do nothing while it is on. Dragging and the wheel still pan and zoom the map. The selection panel and the chat panel are hidden. Draft and committed order lines are not drawn. Replay does not start while a turn is resolving or its playback is running, and says so: "Replay is available once this turn finishes resolving." A match with no recorded turn says "This match has no recorded turns to replay yet."
- **The slider previews as you drag.** The map, the top bar, and the panels follow the turn under the thumb, at most once every 250 ms (`REPLAY_SLIDER_PREVIEW_INTERVAL_MS`). The turn's dialogs open when you let go. The arrow keys, Home, End, Page Up, and Page Down move the slider, and P, Escape, C, and the map's zoom and pan keys still act from it.
- **Play shows the animations.** Each turn shows with its two dialogs for half a second (`REPLAY_PLAYBACK_DWELL_MS`, 500 ms). Then the movement and combat that led into the next turn run, built from that turn's recorded result the way the live turn built them. Play stops at the last recorded turn. While it runs, Play becomes Pause, which stops after the turn that is moving. The step buttons, the slider, and the turn field wait. P or Escape stops it at once and returns to the present. A step that cannot be loaded ends playback and says "That turn could not be loaded."
- **Replay inside a battle.** P in a tactical battle replays that battle's beats and nothing else. The label reads "Replay · Beat N of M", the turn field takes a beat number, and Play runs each beat's marches, fire, melee, and dice. Exit Battle is disabled until you leave replay, and leaving returns to the current beat with its queued marches. From the strategic map, replay never shows a battle.
- **Follow units.** The magnifying-glass button beside the arrow turns follow units on. The map then fits the visible units after every step you show, and after every step Play shows once its animation ends. Click it again to stop. The choice is kept until the app closes.

### Charts

- **Open them with C.** Press **C** during a match or at game over, or check **Charts…** in the game menu. C, Escape, the close control, and a click on the backdrop close the dialog. It shows every side and every turn, whatever fog of war is set to. No commander is shown any of it. The dialog does not open over a dialog that needs your choice.
- **Relationships.** One card for each side an AI commander reports on, headed "How they see you" for your side and "How they see" and the side's name for the others. A commander never reports on its own side, so with one AI opponent only your side has a card. The graph shows each commander around the subject. The line's color is the commander's stance, its width is trust, and a dashed line is a report more than three turns old (`RELATIONSHIP_OLD_AFTER_TURNS`) or none. A commander that has not reported shows "no report". The table under it lists Stance, Trust, Threat, Taken, and As of. Trust and Threat run from 0 to 4 (`RELATIONSHIP_SCALE_MAX`). Taken counts the hexes that commander took from the subject in the last 5 turns (`HEXES_TAKEN_WINDOW_TURNS`).
- **Stance over time.** A strip for the side you pick in the "How they see" dropdown, with a row for each commander and a cell for each turn. A solid cell is a stance reported that turn, a lighter cell carries an earlier stance forward, and an empty outline is no report.
- **Nine line charts.** Built units, Mercenary units, Hexes held, Production per turn, Stored production points, Credits, Upkeep per turn, Units lost per turn, and Model cost so far (USD). Each side has one line in its seat color, and a missing turn is a gap. Built units has a dropdown for Total, Infantry, Armor, Naval, Air, or Missile, and Mercenary units has one for Total, Infantry, Armor, Naval, or Air.
- **Zoom and pan.** Every chart opens on its newest 12 turns (`INITIAL_VISIBLE_TURNS`). Hover for each side's value at that turn and their total. Scroll to zoom, drag to move along a zoomed chart, and double-click to show every turn again. The dialog refreshes when a strategic turn resolves.

### What is recorded each turn

- **Statistics.** After every strategic turn the game records a `side_statistics` event for each side: built units by type, mercenaries by type, hexes held, production per turn, stored points, credits, upkeep, units lost, and model cost so far (`recordTurnStatistics`). Units lost is a difference from the previous turn. A missile that was launched does not count as lost. It is empty for the first turn after a load.
- **Who destroyed what.** A `losses_attributed` event records, for each capture of losses, which side was responsible for each lost unit, for strategic turns, battle beats, and units a battle deletes from the world map.
- **What replay needs.** The recording also holds `view_snapshot` events (the screen's snapshot) and `resolution_view` events (the result the screen received). The recording file written when a match ends carries them too. `npm run recording:summary` still prints a summary of a recording file.
- **Nothing here goes to the commanders.** No prompt contains another side's statistics, stance, trust, or threat, with fog of war on or off.

### Commanders report trust and threat

- **Two scores with each stance.** In the diplomacy step, a commander now gives trust and threat with each stance. Each is a whole number from 0 to 4. The talk prompt says: "With each stance give trust, from 0 to 4, for how far you believe what that side tells you, and threat, from 0 to 4, for how dangerous that side is to you now. No other commander is told your stance, trust, or threat. Base all three on what a side has done to you, as the loss lines in Recent Turn Notes and the Record column show, more than on what it says."
- **The reply format.** Each entry of `stances` in the `talk_reply` object is now `{"toward":"<side code>","stance":"<hostile|wary|neutral|cordial|friendly>","trust":<0-4>,"threat":<0-4>,"reason":"<one sentence>"}`. When the model supports structured output, `trust` and `threat` are required integers, limited to 0 through 4 by an enum. A score that is missing or outside 0 to 4 is stored as empty, and the stance is kept.
- **A commander sees its own scores.** In the Sides table of the talk prompt, a stored stance reads `{stance} (trust {t}, threat {h}): {reason}`. In the orders prompt's Messages section, each line of "Your stances and records:" (or "Your stances:") reads `- {label} ({code}): {stance}, trust {t}, threat {h}. {reason}`. A score the commander never gave reads `not reported`.
- **Scores are private.** A commander sees only its own. The charts are where you see all of them.

### Ships need sea access

- **The rule.** A ship needs water or coast in the hex or next to it. At a seaport with only land around it, you cannot build ships, raise a naval mercenary, or board (`hasSeaAccess`). Unloading at such a port is still allowed, and a ship already in the queue there is still built.
- **What you see.** The hex tooltip's Features line reads "Seaports (no sea access)". The build popup leaves Naval out of its list there. Changing the count of a naval row already queued there is refused with "Ships cannot be built here: this seaport has no water or coast next to it." Applying a multi-hex build template skips a naval row at such a seaport.
- **What the commanders are told.** The Production rules line reads `naval cost 100 (min urban 10 + min seaports 1 + water or coast in or next to the hex)`. The Transport section reads "Embark: coastal, a land seaport with water or coast next to it (control not required), or a water seaport you control." The coaching line says "loading requires a coastal cell, a land seaport with water or coast next to it, or a controlled seaport on transit water". The briefing's production queues no longer list Naval at a port without sea access. A `set_build_queue` call for `naval` at one is refused with the same sentence the build popup shows. The engine's sea operations use the same boarding test.

### Game Options during a match

- **Settings opens Game Options.** The top bar's **Settings** button opens Game Options as a dialog centered over the window, with the live match's settings. It does not open over the new-game screen, the charts, or another dialog.
- **What you can change.** While you plan a turn on the world map: Victory rule, Turn limit, AI-only battles, Beat limit, Sight cues, and the four bonuses (Origin, Tech, Terrain, Weather). Click **Apply**. The change takes effect from the next turn's resolution, and an AI consult already running keeps the old rules.
- **What stays fixed.** Nickname, Fog of war, and Auto-start AI show the match's values, disabled. At any other time every control is disabled, the dialog says "These are the settings in force. They can be changed while you plan a turn on the world map.", and Cancel reads Close.
- **Turn limit.** Only limits no earlier than the current turn are offered. A limit equal to the current turn ends the match after that turn resolves. A limit that has already passed moves to the first one still offered.

### Menu and layout

- **The game menu** now lists New game…, Save game…, Load game…, Playback…, Charts…, Offer tactical battles, How to play, and Quit. Playback… and Charts… are checkboxes, with P and C shown at the right. They follow the key presses.
- **How to play opens the guide.** It opens the How to play guide on GitHub in your web browser. When the browser cannot be opened, a toast says "The How to play guide could not be opened in the browser."
- **The scale and the chat panel.** The map scale now sits at the horizontal center of the map's bottom edge. The chat panel's bottom edge is level with the selection panel's. On a map narrower than about 1024 px the panels draw over the scale.

### Running out of credits

- **A 402 fails fast for 60 seconds.** After OpenRouter answers a request with an insufficient-credits 402, every AI order request, for any seat, fails at once for the next 60 seconds (`OPENROUTER_CREDITS_EXHAUSTED_FAST_FAIL_MS`). It writes "OpenRouter account has insufficient credits. Add credits at https://openrouter.ai/settings/credits and try again." to the AI activity log without building a prompt. After that window, the next request makes one real attempt, so adding credits works without restarting. Saving any API key ends the window at once.

## Known limits

- There is no in-app list of saves. A finished match still writes a history file, and the app does not open it. Replay reads the match you have open.
- Replay and the charts begin at the first turn this version recorded. Neither shows the turns of a save from before 3.1.0. Nothing is recorded when `AGENT_WARS_DISABLE_RECORDER` is set.
- Replay never shows a battle from the strategic map, and it does not draw a past turn's order lines or the last-seen enemy markers. It does not start while a turn is resolving, and it does not show the AI's tool log for a past turn.
- Help, the Build and Forces tabs, and the Replay button on a turn report in the Reports tab are placeholders in this version. Settings and How to play work.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- On the world map, outside a battle, a route or a played-back move that crosses the 180° meridian runs off the edge of the map. The destination hex is drawn on the opposite edge.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
