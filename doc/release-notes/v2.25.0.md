A turn-based grand strategy game on a real-Earth hex map. Your opponents are large language models you choose through OpenRouter. Coding agents wrote the code; the story is told in the Agent Wars series in [*Standing Orders*](https://standingorders.substack.com).

## Downloads

| File | For |
| --- | --- |
| `agent-wars-2.25.0-windows-x64.zip` | Windows, x64 |
| `agent-wars-2.25.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS on Apple silicon |
| `agent-wars-2.25.0-linux-x64.tar.gz` | Linux, x64 |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | SHA-256 checksums for the three archives |

These are unsigned side-load builds, so Windows and macOS warn before the first launch.

- **Windows:** unzip, then run `Agent Wars v2.25.0.exe`. If SmartScreen stops it, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** unzip, then open `Agent Wars v2.25.0.app`. If macOS blocks it, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Linux:** extract the archive, then run `./agent-wars`. That launcher handles Chromium's sandbox when the archive can't keep `chrome-sandbox` owned by root. On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, where AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces, it starts without the sandbox and says so.

To check a download, compare its SHA-256 with the line in `SHA256SUMS.txt`: `Get-FileHash <file>` in PowerShell, `shasum -a 256 <file>` on macOS, or `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` on Linux.

## Before you play

- **Bring your own OpenRouter key.** Start a match. The console on the right starts collapsed; click **Command** and open a commander's card. Paste that opponent's key, choose a model, and click **Start**. Each opponent has its own key. A new game fills only the first opponent from the key you used last. OpenRouter bills your account for the commanders' model calls. Without a key and a model on at least one commander the app still runs, **Start** stays off, and that opponent makes no model calls. The key stays on your computer and is sent only to OpenRouter ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).
- **The map is in the download.** Shaded relief is packed with the app, so the map does not use a tile server. The first time a regional map is shown, its tiles unpack on this computer. A commander's model calls still need an internet connection.
- **Non-commercial use.** The app ships earth data under non-commercial terms, so the application as distributed is for non-commercial use. See [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
- **Saves.** A save from 2.12.0 through 2.24.0 still opens. A save from 2.10.0 or earlier will not load in 2.25.0.

How a match plays is in [doc/how-to-play.md](https://github.com/michael-kitchin/agent-wars/blob/main/doc/how-to-play.md).

## What's in 2.25.0

### Production

- **Production Priorities.** The production briefing has a new `Production Priorities` subsection, between Paused Queues and Controlled Hex Queues (`buildProductionPriorityLines` in `src/main/openRouter/productionPriorities.ts`). It names at most one city each for a missile, a ship, and credits, in that order, and leaves out any line whose condition fails. A candidate city must have city cells, list that item as buildable, and not be paused. Build time is the item's cost, less any stored points, divided by the city's urban cells, and never under one turn. Stored points count only when the queue is empty or that item is next.
  - The missile line needs fewer missiles than the cap and none already queued. It picks the explored enemy city with the most city cells that a builder's missile could reach (`missileReachSet`), then the builder that finishes soonest: `- Missile: <city> can build one in <N> turns and reaches <target>, a <owner> city with <N> city cells.`
  - The naval line needs fewer ships than half the naval cap, rounded up (`NAVAL_PRIORITY_CAP_SHARE`, 0.5), none already queued, and at least one explored enemy city on the coast. It names the builder that finishes soonest: `- Naval: <city> can build one in <N> turns. <N> enemy cities are on the coast, where a ship can shell them and land ground units.`
  - The credits line needs a mercenary economy, no queue with credits next, and a treasury under ten times the urban floor below (`CREDITS_TREASURY_CEILING_TURNS`). It names the smallest city with at least 12 urban cells on Small, 24 on Medium, or 36 on Large (`CREDITS_CITY_MIN_URBAN_PER_SIZE` times `GAME_SIZE_MULTIPLIER`): `- Credits: no city is producing credits. Queue credits in <city>.`

- **Credits city.** The production coaching bullet now tells every commander: "Keep one city queued on credits at all times. Credits are the only way to raise mercenaries, and mercenaries are the only units you can place away from your own cities." It then points at the new subsection: "Production Priorities, under Production Status, names a city for a missile, a naval unit, or credits when one is worth building. Queue what it names before filling the other cities."

### What commanders are told

- **Ships and missiles before you own them.** On a strategic consult with production on, `seatBuildCapabilities` checks whether any city the side controls can still queue a ship or a missile, below the cap. If one can, the commander reads the naval rules, or a missile build sentence, before it owns that arm. The missile build sentence is: "A missile is built in a city with at least <N> urban cells. It never moves and is spent when it launches. It reaches any explored hex within <N> hexes of the hex where it was built." It also says what a hit destroys, and it does not mention `missileLaunches`. A side that owns a missile keeps the launch sentence. A battle still shows these rules only when that arm is in the fight.

- **Your tools.** The strategic context now has a `Your tools are:` list after the strategy lines (`buildStrategicToolsBlock`). Each line appears only for an arm the side can use. Missiles, when the side owns or can build one: "Missiles destroy city cells, airports, and seaports up to <N> hexes away, beyond the reach of aircraft. Build one in a city that reaches a large enemy city, then launch it." Ships, when it owns or can build one: they shell any hex within 2 (`getRange('naval')`) and carry ground units to an open coast. Aircraft, when it has one: they strike within 3 hexes of their airport (`AIR_STRIKE_RANGE_HEXES`), carry one infantry unit between airports, and drop it within 3 hexes. Mercenaries, when production is on and the match has a mercenary economy: "Mercenaries are raised for credits in any explored city outside an enemy home region that holds no enemy unit. They do not count toward unit caps. Raise them beside the campaign target, or behind the enemy to split its force." Without a campaign, that line says "your objective".

- **Goals.** The strategic goals list grew. With the region-versus-region win paths it has six goals, up from five. The second is now "Bring more force to each fight than the enemy can. Mass your ground units before an attack, and use ships, aircraft, and mercenaries to put units where the enemy is weak." A new third goal reads "Stop the enemy doing the same. Destroy the city cells, airports, and seaports that build and move its units." The city-building goal now reads "Keep every city building, and keep credits coming in for mercenaries," in place of banking credits at the unit caps. Without those win paths the list has four goals, up from three, and the new third goal is "Destroy the city cells, airports, and seaports that build and move enemy units."

- **Strike production.** A strategic consult adds one doctrine line for balanced and cautious commanders (`buildStrategicDoctrineBlock`). Balanced gets "(3) Strike the cities, airports, and seaports your ground units cannot reach. Production the enemy loses is force it cannot bring." Cautious gets "(4) Strike enemy cities, airports, and seaports from range before you commit ground units." Aggressive already had a production line and is unchanged. A battle does not add either line.

- **Launch your missiles.** A side that owns a missile gets a new coaching bullet: "Launch every missile that Missile Forces lists a target for. Copy the first target row that names the missile into missileLaunches. A missile that is never launched destroys nothing." Attention Flags also gets a missile line built from the first Missile Forces target row: `Missiles: <ids> can launch. Best target: <hex>, a <owner> city with <N> city cells. Add a missileLaunches entry.`

### Mercenaries

- **Where to raise.** The Raise Options table has three new columns: `To Campaign Target` (the hex distance, or `—` without a campaign), `Enemy Units Within 2` (`RAISE_OPTIONS_ENEMY_RADIUS`), and `Chokepoint` (`yes` at three or fewer passable land neighbors, `CHOKEPOINT_MAX_PASSABLE_NEIGHBORS`). The table still shows up to ten rows, in the same order.

- **Raise attention line.** Attention Flags now names one raise the treasury can pay for, even when the table cut that hex off (`formatMercenaryRaiseAttention` in `src/main/openRouter/mercenaryRaiseAttention.ts`): `Mercenaries: your treasury of <N> credits can raise <type> at <hex> for <fee>. Add a raise_mercenaries row to productionOrders.` A raise counts as affordable when the credits cover the fee plus three turns of that unit's upkeep (`MERCENARY_UPKEEP_RESERVE_TURNS`). The line takes the first affordable hex in table order outside the side's home region, or the first affordable hex at home when none is outside. It names the cheapest affordable type there, with ties going infantry, armor, naval, then air. The missile and mercenary lines sit after the production and campaign bullets and do not count against the five unit flags. A battle shows neither.

- **Mercenaries section at zero credits.** A side with no credits, no mercenaries, and nobody to rehire used to see only `Treasury: 0 credits. No one to rehire.` It now sees the full Mercenaries section when any hex can still raise.

## Known limits

- There is no in-app list of saves, and no turn replay.
- Settings, Help, How to play, the Build and Forces tabs, and Replay on a turn report are placeholders in this version.
- On macOS, Quit closes the window and the app stays in the dock.
- On the world map, outside a battle, a route or a played-back move that crosses the 180° meridian runs off the edge of the map. The destination hex is drawn on the opposite edge.
- The macOS build is for Apple silicon only, and it hasn't been opened on a Mac yet, so its first-launch steps are untested.
- Model quality varies. A model without tool calls plays without the planning tools.
- This is mostly a personal R&D project. Issues and pull requests are welcome; responses and acceptance are best-effort. Security reports go through the repository's **Security** tab.
