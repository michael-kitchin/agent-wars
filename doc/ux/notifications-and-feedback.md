# Notifications and Feedback

Which surface reports which kind of message, including the two map toasts.

## Purpose

Keep errors, turn summaries, and the opponent's consultation text on the surfaces that already show them, so a new message does not invent a fourth channel.

## Feedback Channels

| Channel | Used For | Lifetime | Details In |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Map toast | Errors, rejected orders, turn-loss summaries, and basemap load failure | Until dismiss, or until a fixed interval elapses. A new map toast replaces the old one and restarts the interval. The basemap failure uses the same interval. | This document |
| Reports tab | A copy of every informational map toast, and one Message entry per kept commander dispatch. The message dialog is not filed | Accumulates during a match. Starting a new match clears it. | [reports-tab.md](reports-tab.md) |
| AI strategy toast | One tab per AI opponent. A tab starts disabled and turns on when that opponent sends a message or a strategy, or when this appearance can say why that opponent was not consulted. The selected tab shows Message, then Strategy, whenever that consult sent them | Until dismiss, or until the same fixed interval elapses. Each new message restarts that interval and selects its tab. A click on an enabled tab restarts that interval. | This document |
| Sidebar error line | Nothing. It is not a player-visible surface. Errors reported to the sidebar, such as a failed Ready or a failed tactical commit, show on the map toast instead. | Always hidden | This document |
| AI activity log | AI interaction lines and error lines, including a one-line copy of a turn-update toast | Accumulates during a match. Starting a new match clears it. | [ai-activity-log.md](ai-activity-log.md) |
| Hex tooltips | The hex readout, whose Effects line includes movement, range, and cover when those apply, a blocked-order notice, the terrain, weather, and target cover that change an aimed order, and, on a legal ranged aim, the opponents on the target plus Kill, Hurt, and Loss | The hex readout and the effects notice appear one second after the pointer rests. Moving farther than 6 pixels before either opens starts that wait again. Once open, the hex readout stays while the pointer remains on that cell. The effects notice stays too, until the player stops aiming. The blocked notice appears one second after the pointer rests and follows the pointer, then hides itself a short time after the pointer stops. Moving farther than 6 pixels before it opens starts that wait again. Stopping the aim, changing cell, leaving the map, or pressing hides it, including a notice that has not opened. A control tip hides the hex readout when that control tip appears. | [hex-tooltips.md](hex-tooltips.md) |
| Control tips | Why the player would use a button, text field, checkbox, or dropdown, and what they can enter | Appears one second after the pointer or keyboard focus arrives on the control or its label. Moving on that same control does not start the wait again. Hides when the pointer and focus leave, when the control is hidden, when a dropdown list opens or a choice is made, or while a blocked or effects order tip is showing. | [control-tooltips.md](control-tooltips.md) |
| Bonuses tooltip | The unit's name and formation date when those are known, then a Mercenary line when the unit is a mercenary, then its birth bonuses: Origin, Terrain, and Weather when those options are on, and Tech when the tech bonus is on and the unit is Basic or Advanced | Appears one second after the pointer arrives on that unit flag or one-unit token, and stays while the pointer remains. Moving on the flag or token does not start that wait again. Hides when the pointer leaves. | [stack-callout.md](stack-callout.md) |
| Stack callout | Units in a hex | Until the player dismisses it or the next map gesture closes it | [stack-callout.md](stack-callout.md) |

## Availability

The map toast and the AI strategy toast can appear in Strategic planning, Resolution playback, and Tactical planning. They do not require a mode change. While the new-game overlay is open, those toasts stay without pointer input, though the map under the setup panel can be dragged. The message toast sits in the map's band. It stays to the right of the setup panel unless the opponent tab row is wider than that gap, and then the band overlaps the panel so the tabs stay on one line. The map toast sits at the lower right. A tall setup panel is stacked above it.

## Information Displayed

- The message toast shows in a band at the top of the map, 12 px below the map's top edge. `layoutMapChrome` in `src/renderer/map/mapChromeLayout.ts` sets that band's width from the map. While the message toast is hidden, the width is the gap `toastBandSpan` leaves in `src/shared/mapChromePlacement.ts`, and no wider than one third of the visible map. The visible map is the area beside the console, so that third is smaller while the console is open and larger while the console is collapsed to the rail. While the message toast is showing, `messageToastBandWidth` keeps that width when the tab row fits, and widens the band to the tab row when it does not. The band never grows past the map minus the 12 px inset on each side. Its right edge stays 12 px in from the map's right edge, with the same gap while the console is open and while it is collapsed to the rail. It stays to the right of the minimap panel, or of the terrain legend when that legend is open beside the panel, or of the new-game setup panel when that panel reaches farther, except when the tab row is wider than that gap. Then the band overlaps that chrome so the tabs stay on one line. When the space between that left chrome and the right inset is narrower than 280 px, as at the 800 × 600 minimum window, the toast may overlap that chrome even with a short tab row. Switching opponent tabs does not change that width, because every opponent's tab is already in the row. The map toast is separate, at the map's lower right, 12 px from the right edge and 12 px from the bottom, on the same corner as the + and − buttons, and it covers them. It is at least as tall as that control. It is only as wide as its text and no wider than one third of the map. A toast wider than that control also covers the scale on the same bottom line. Where it meets the selection panel, it draws above the panel. Both use the navy chrome in the [UI style guide](../ui-style-guide.md). An error has a light-red border. An informational message, including a turn update, a basemap failure, and the AI strategy toast, has the steel border. The body is the message text, not a fixed sentence list.
- A turn-update toast leads with one section per side that lost a visible unit, lost hexes, controlled a hex hit by a visible missile launch, or owned a hex whose fortifications fell (`buildTurnLossSections` in `src/shared/turnLossSections.ts`): `Your Losses` for the viewer, then `{name} Losses` for every other seat in seat order, then `Neutral Losses`. The name is `sideDisplayName` in `src/shared/sideNicknames.ts`. A null nickname shows `Human` for the human seat and the short home name for an AI seat. A header and a hex sentence use that same name. `Your Losses` stays the viewer's header. An AI seat with a blank home name uses the seat id in that header and in a hex sentence. When two seats show the same name, the lower `seatOrder` is the side that header and that hex sentence belong to (`lossSectionOwnerId`). Cause lines come first. A side that lost a strategic unit in a battle adds one line after those cause lines and before the missile and hex sentences (`formatDestroyedInBattleLine` in `src/shared/destroyedInBattleLine.ts`). It names the units in the order they were destroyed, then a group when a type has more than three, and it ends with `destroyed in battle.` A group reads `6x Armored`, in naval, air, armored, infantry, then missile order. A type with three or fewer stays named. A side whose only loss is that line still gets a section. The line is not a cause, and the next turn does not repeat it. A battle Ready opened holds that turn's popup until the battle ends and the Ready that follows has returned. That held popup is the one left on the map, and its report tag is the turn that opened the battle. It keeps that turn's View as, seat names, and explored hexes. Ending the match, or starting another, drops a popup that is still waiting. A battle opened from the map adds its units to the next strategic update. Another battle before that update adds its units too, and does not drop the ones already waiting. A start that fails shows that turn's popup at once. It does not add units for the battle that did not start. Units already waiting from an earlier battle stay on that popup. All sides lists every destroyed parent. A seat lists its own, and an enemy parent whose hex that seat could see. A land unit removed because its carrier was destroyed is on the same line. One missile sentence follows the battle line, or the cause lines when that side has no battle line. It totals every visible strike on hexes that side controlled: `Missile strike in AA destroyed 1 city cell.` or `Missile strikes in AA and C2 destroyed 3 city cells, 1 airport, and 2 fortifications; 2 squadrons lost.` The word is `strike` for one launch and `strikes` for more than one, including two launches on one hex. Only kinds with a destroyed total above 0 are named, in city cell, airport, seaport, fortification order. The squadron clause is `; 1 squadron lost`, or `; N squadrons lost` when more than one was lost, and it is added only when at least one squadron was lost. When every total is 0 it ends `destroyed nothing.` (`buildMissileStrikeToastLines` in `src/shared/missileStrikeReport.ts`). Strikes on neutral or uncontrolled hexes share one such sentence in Updates, after deployments or the combat-activity fallback, and before the unknown-battle line. One fortification sentence (`buildFortificationToastLines` in `src/shared/fortificationReport.ts`) follows that side's missile sentence and comes before its hex sentences: `Fortifications in AA and C2 absorbed 3 hits.`, or `hit.` for one. It does not say how many remain. Both sentences name hexes by code, as the hex tooltip does (`orderTargetHexCode`): `AA` on the world map and `AA.B0` for a battle cell. Codes are listed once each, sorted alphabetically, and a hex without a code is named `an unidentified hex`, last (`formatTurnReportHexList` in `src/shared/turnReportHexCodes.ts`). A battle beat report has the same fortification sentence for its cells that absorbed a hit that beat, filed under the hex controller, for example `Fortifications in AA.B0 and AA.B9 absorbed 2 hits.` (`absorbedReports` on `createTacticalFortificationHooks`). Every side sees it, because a battle has no fog. A battle has no missile sentence. A Neutral-owned one sits under Neutral Losses. Hex sentences (`hexChangeLinesByOwner` in `src/shared/turnReportLines.ts`) are last in that section, after a missile sentence when that side has one. A hex sentence reads `{Name} lost {N} hex to {others}.` when `{N}` is one, and `{Name} lost {N} hexes to {others}.` otherwise. `{N}` is that side's total for the turn, written as digits. `{others}` names each other owner once. The viewer is `You` as the subject and `you` in a list, and `you` is first. That viewer is `reportViewerSeatId` in `src/renderer/gameplay/readyResolutionAnnouncements.ts`: the active human seat, the View as seat while observing, or none while View as is All sides (`readoutViewerSeatId` in `src/shared/observeModeControls.ts`). The rest follow `localeCompare('en')` of the short name, with seat order breaking a tie, then the owner id. Two names use `and`. Three or more use commas and a final `and`. A transition counts only when both controllers are set and they differ. Deployments and caps are the Updates lines (`buildOtherTurnUpdatePrimaryLines`). The combat-activity fallback is used only when there are no side sections and none of those lines. While there is no viewer, every side's header uses its full name, and hex sentences use short names with no `You`. An opponent's name in a loss header, and each opponent's short name in a hex sentence, is preceded by a flag of that opponent's index, 1 through 4, on the side's map color, with a white digit (`aiIndexForSeatId` in `src/shared/seatIds.ts`). The short name stays after that flag, as in the flag then `N. Africa`. A name the ordinary cut would reduce to a single initial, or a cut that leaves an unclosed parenthesis, uses the letter form of the full home name, such as SECT or TVTNZ. A short form longer than the full name is not used. A trailing abbreviation in parentheses is left off that short name. Two opponents that share a header's displayed name use the lower seat order. Two that share a short name in one hex sentence each get a flag: the subject is that section's owner, and the list follows the sentence. Your Losses, Neutral Losses, `You`, `you`, and Neutral have no number. When combat was outside vision, Updates ends with one last line. That line is `formatUnknownBattleAnnouncement` in `src/shared/unknownBattleAnnouncement.ts`. Names are trimmed, repeated names are dropped, and the rest are sorted with `localeCompare('en')` without folding case, then joined with commas: `Unknown battle observed in Africa, Europe.` One name uses that same sentence. No name left, including a failed lookup, reads `Unknown battle observed in an unidentified continent.` The sentence is part of the turn report. There is no second Notice for it.
- In a turn-update toast, a named victim is the unit name without a side-name prefix, preceded by that unit's origin flag. The same display name on two sides still gets each side's flag. The flag uses the same Bonuses tooltip as the [stack callout](stack-callout.md). The casualty carries the unit's birth record (`RemovedUnitSnapshot.origin` in `src/shared/ipc/readyTypes.ts`, copied by `withLossFlagFacts` in `src/shared/lossFlagFacts.ts`), so a unit the player had not seen on the map still shows the flag when a record exists. A unit with no birth record shows no flag. The tooltip keeps the formation date, weather tags, and tech tier carried with that record, including a Tech line when the tech bonus is on and the tier is known. If two victims of one side share a display name but would not show the same flag and the same formation date, that name gets no flag for that side. Cause labels, in order, are `Ranged attack`, `Melee attack`, `Return fire`, `Air strike`, `Missile strike`, and `Other losses` (`LOSS_CAUSE_LABELS` in `src/shared/lossSummary.ts`). A missile that launched is not one of those losses. A type with more than three losses on one cause line collapses to a count such as `4x Infantry` (`LOSS_TYPE_NAME_LIST_MAX` in `src/shared/lossSummary.ts`). Named units come first. Groups follow in naval, air, armored, then infantry order. A type with three or fewer stays named, and a type that is not one of those four stays named. The destroyed-in-battle line uses that same flag, the same tooltip, and the same type icon. Its collapsed count uses the same `x`, such as `6x Armored`, and missile is a group on that line. The commander's prompt is unchanged and still groups a whole line past five as `6 Infantry`. It does not list units destroyed in battle. A collapsed group has one type icon in the losing side's map color, with a white glyph and no origin flag. The Updates section and every other toast line have no origin flags.
- A count of one uses the singular: "1 infantry unit deployed" in Updates, and "1 hex" in a hex sentence. Two or more keep the plural, so one armor and one infantry read "1 armor and 1 infantry units deployed" (`buildOtherTurnUpdatePrimaryLines` in `src/shared/consolidatedTurnUpdateToast.ts`). After deployments and fortifications, Updates adds `formatCreditsAddedLine`, `formatMercenariesHiredLine`, `formatUnitsDesertedLine`, and `formatHireOrdersUnfilledLine` when those counts are not zero, then the deployment-cap line. "20 credits added to the treasury." "1 mercenary hired." "1 unit deserted for lack of pay." "1 hire order could not be filled, so it was not charged." Queues that paused this turn get one sentence per set of reasons, naming hexes by code (`formatQueuePausedLines`), before the unknown-battle line: "Queues paused at AA and C2: occupying units or fortifications required." The reasons are `occupying units or fortifications required`, `every city cell fortified`, and `unit cap reached`. Two are joined with `and`, and three with a comma and a final `and` (`joinWithSerialComma`). The next item is one type, so a full city and the unit cap do not appear together. One hex reads "Queue paused at". A queue that stays paused is not named again until it builds and then pauses again. `queuePauseUpdateLinesFor` builds those lines from `humanNewlyPausedQueues`.
- A basemap failure is a local tile read. It uses the map toast once, and the text is `Some map tiles failed to load. The game remains playable; try again later.` It hides after the same interval as other map toasts, on dismiss, or when a later map toast replaces it. A later tile failure in the same session does not show it again.
- The AI strategy toast is a tab for each AI opponent, including one who has been knocked out, labeled with that side's name from `sideDisplayName` (`src/shared/sideNicknames.ts`). A null nickname shows the short home name, or the seat id when that home is blank, and ordered by opponent number, 1 through 4 (`aiIndexForSeatId`). The tab row does not wrap. A label that does not fit clips with an ellipsis, and the full name is the button's tooltip. Each message-tab flag uses the same badge as every other opponent flag (`1.125em` by `0.75em`, the shared black-and-white drop shadow, and a centered digit at `0.65em` with `translateY(0.12em)`, from `.side-number-flag` in `static/overlayChrome.css`). The tab is a button, so that badge uses the button's `-0.05em` offset, the same as an origin flag in an Orders row. Every tab starts disabled. A message enables that opponent's tab. A strategy does too, including while a human seat is playing. That arrival replaces the tab's body and selects it. The selected panel shows Message, then Strategy, whenever that consult sent them. Each tab, including a disabled tab, starts with that opponent's number flag. The tab fill is the popup background, `--chrome-bg`. The selected tab stays bold, with a teal underline. A click on an enabled tab, including the tab that is already selected, shows that opponent's tab and restarts the 24 second timer. A new message still restarts the timer and selects that tab. Dismiss, the timer, selecting a unit, opening the build popup, arming ranged, air, or missile-launch targeting, and entering a battle hide the toast. The next message builds the tabs again, disabled until that message. When this appearance did not consult a seat, that tab is enabled with one Message line and no Strategy section (`formatCommanderAbsenceMessage` in `src/shared/commanderMessageTabs.ts`): knocked out, no commander model, no reply in time, the consult failed, no new consult was required, not in this battle, or no units left in this battle. Not in this battle is used only when the opposing party's latest consult is that battle (`seatOutsideTacticalConsult` in `src/shared/commanderMessageTabs.ts`). A strategic consult that finishes while a battle is still open keeps its own line. A seat that is still planning stays disabled. A consult that finished without prose stays disabled. The line does not select the tab when another opponent already sent a message or a strategy. It is not a Reports entry. The dialog stays closed when every line would be one of those reasons. The shared toast is not filed. Each kept dispatch is its own Reports entry, including a strategy-only consult. A stale or abandoned consult is filed and does not open the dialog.
- A knockout uses the map toast. The text is one notice per seat (`announceKnockouts` in `src/renderer/gameplay/knockoutNotices.ts`). The Reports tab already has one Notice per seat, so that toast is not filed again.
- The sidebar error line shows nothing and stays hidden.

## Inputs and Responses

### Mouse

- When the player clicks the map toast dismiss control, the map toast hides and its hide timer is cancelled.
- While the map toast is showing, a click on it stays on the toast, including where it covers the + and − buttons, the scale, or the selection panel. The wheel still zooms, toward the pointer (`bindMapToastWheel` in `src/renderer/openRouter/mapToastWheel.ts`). When the toast can still scroll in that direction, the wheel scrolls the toast instead. While the new-game overlay is open, the toast takes no pointer input, so the wheel and those buttons reach the map under it.
- When the player clicks the AI strategy toast dismiss control, that toast hides and its hide timer is cancelled.

### Keyboard

- None.

### Other

- When a new map toast is shown, it replaces the previous map toast.
- When an informational map toast is shown, the [Reports tab](reports-tab.md) files a copy, unless the caller already filed that notice. An error toast is not filed. The message dialog is not filed. Each kept commander dispatch is already its own Message entry. An absence line on a message tab is not a Reports entry. A stale or abandoned consult is filed and does not open the dialog.
- When an error is reported to the sidebar, the map toast shows that text as an error. The sidebar error line stays hidden.

## States

- Hidden: the toast is not shown. This is the start state, the state after dismiss, and the state after the hide interval.
- Visible: a message is showing. A newer message of the same toast replaces the body and restarts the interval.
- The sidebar error line has only the hidden state.

## Invariants

- Errors are reported on the map toast only. The sidebar error line never shows text.
- Every map toast, including the basemap failure, hides after the fixed interval unless a newer message replaced it first.
- An empty AI strategy toast never stays visible.
- Showing one toast does not hide the other toast.
- Every informational map toast is filed on the Reports tab unless that notice was already filed. An unknown battle is part of that turn report, not a second Notice. Each kept commander dispatch is filed once, from the pool. The message dialog is not filed. An absence line on a message tab is not a Reports entry. An error toast never is.

## Strategic and Tactical Differences

Both toasts use the same show, replace, and dismiss behavior in both theaters. Tactical Ready and strategic Ready can both raise a turn-update map toast and an AI strategy toast.
